What you get

Users describe problems in public in detail: exact error messages, versions, configurations and the steps that triggered them. That signal is spread across forums, issue trackers and help centers, often repeated across many threads. Thread Witness gathers it into:

  • A weekly report with the top issues and top feature requests, each with a unique reporter count, trend, severity and status. Every topic keeps the same ID from week to week, so the ID in your ticket matches the one in next quarter’s review.
  • Deep dives per issue: symptoms, exact error strings, triggering conditions, an affected-environment matrix, a timeline annotated with your releases, and the workarounds users tried.
  • Workaround counts: how many people said a step worked, how many said it did not, and what it costs to apply.
  • Release correlation, and fix verification on Business and Enterprise plans: whether reports rose after a release, and whether they fell after a fix shipped.
  • Evidence you can check: every factual sentence links to the public post it came from.
  • In development: what users praise, grouped beside what they report; spike alerts to Slack, Microsoft Teams or email; and issues pushed to Jira, Linear or GitHub with the ID and evidence attached.

How it works and how we source data

We read public sources through official APIs and feeds first, merge reports of the same problem into one topic, count unique reporters, and have an automated verifier check every citation, quote and number before a report is released. How it works

Public data only, no logins bypassed, review sites and retailer reviews excluded, Reddit read only under license, authors pseudonymized, quotes short and linked. Counts measure public discussion, not failure rates. How we source data · Corrections
